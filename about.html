Yuge Wang

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Political Science and International Affairs at the University of Georgia and expect to receive my Ph.D. in spring 2027. I specialize in comparative politics and political methodology.

My research examines how authoritarian power travels beyond a ruler’s inner circle—down political hierarchies and through governing institutions—to shape political selection, state capacity, and political legitimacy. I develop broader comparative arguments about authoritarian rule and test their implications primarily in China, drawing on original datasets, quantitative analysis, computational text analysis, and survey experiments.

I grew up in Hangzhou, an eastern Chinese city best known for West Lake. Before beginning my doctoral studies, I worked for six years at China Construction Bank and then spent seven years as an assistant professor at the Tourism College of Zhejiang, a teaching-focused institution.

Outside academia, I enjoy reading about history, watching movies, hiking, and playing tennis.
